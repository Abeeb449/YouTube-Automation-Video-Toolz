The Two Orcas That Emptied a Great White Shark Coast
Length: 2,178 words of narration, about 15 to 16 minutes at 140 words a minute.

The script

SCENE 1: HOOK (0:00 to 0:46)
3D footage only in this scene.

In May 2017, a great white shark almost five metres long washed up on a beach in South Africa. A shark
that size has almost no enemies in the sea. Yet one clean tear ran across her chest, and her liver was
gone. Within weeks, three more great whites came ashore with the same wound.

The killers were two orcas that local boat crews already knew by name. Over the next few years, these
two whales helped empty a bay once called the great white shark capital of the world. This is how they
did it, and why scientists still argue over how much of the blame is theirs.

SCENE 2: THE SHARK CAPITAL (0:46 to 1:55)
[B-roll] IMAGE: Map of South Africa's south coast with Cape Town, False Bay, Gansbaai
and Mossel Bay marked. Keywords: "Western Cape coast map", "Gansbaai False Bay
Mossel Bay map", "South Africa south coast map"

The story starts in a small fishing town called Gansbaai, on the coast southeast of Cape Town. A few
kilometres offshore lies Dyer Island, home to a large colony of Cape fur seals. Where there are seals,
great whites follow.

For years, the sharks were here on almost every day of the year, and people travelled to see them. South
Africa protected great whites by law in 1991, the first country to do so. After that, cage diving grew fast.
Along this southern coast, shark tourism came to draw more than 80,000 visitors a year.

Every boat that went out kept a log of each shark its crew saw. Over time, those notes became a
scientific record. Before 2017, the record shows only two times when a week or more passed without a
great white. One gap lasted a week in 2007. The other lasted three weeks in 2016.

In early 2017, the logbooks began to fill with empty days.

SCENE 3: BODIES ON THE BEACH (1:55 to 3:19)
The first body came in February. A young female, 2.6 metres long, lay on a beach near Gansbaai. Her
body was whole, and nobody could say what had killed her.

[B-roll] IMAGE: Real photo of the 4.9 metre female great white on Franskraal beach,
with researchers beside her. Keywords: "4.9m great white shark Franskraal 2017", "great
white shark necropsy Gansbaai 2017", "Alison Towner white shark necropsy"

Then, on the third of May, came the big female from the start of this story. She lay on Franskraal beach.
Biologist Alison Towner and her team from the Dyer Island Conservation Trust measured her at 4.9
metres, about 16 feet. They put her weight at 1,110 kilograms. No larger great white had ever been
dissected in South Africa. Boat crews had last logged her alive at Dyer Island almost a year earlier.

During the examination, the team found the tear between her front fins. Inside, only the liver was
missing.

A male washed up the next day. His liver was gone, and so was his heart. Two more great whites
followed, one later in May and one in June. All four carried the same torn chest. On two of them, the
team found scratches left by orca teeth.

Towner had a good idea who had done it. She had seen this wound before, on a different kind of shark.

SCENE 4: TWO BENT FINS (3:19 to 4:59)
Two years earlier, in False Bay beside Cape Town, divers had a favourite reef. On a good dive they could
count up to 70 of these sevengill sharks there. Sevengills are smaller sharks that hunt close to the seabed. On the
ninth of November 2015, the divers found none.

Over the following days, carcasses lay on the seabed. More washed up in April 2016, and marine
biologist Alison Kock examined five of them. Each one had a torn chest and no liver. Orca teeth had left
marks on the skin. Her work gave the first proof that orcas on this coast were killing sharks for a single
organ.

[B-roll] IMAGE: Real photo of Port and Starboard at the surface, showing their bent
dorsal fins. Keywords: "Port and Starboard orcas", "orca collapsed dorsal fin South
Africa", "Port Starboard killer whales fins"

The suspects were easy to spot, because their tall back fins have folded over. One fin leans to the left and
the other leans to the right. Local boat crews named them Port and Starboard, the sailor's words for left
and right.

Nobody knows how old they are. Early genetic work hints that they may be half-brothers. Sighting
records place them off Lüderitz in Namibia as early as 2009. Since then they have turned up along the
coast as far east as Port Elizabeth.

And the pair had been seen off Gansbaai the day before that first dead shark in February 2017. The next
puzzle was why two orcas would go to such trouble for one organ.

SCENE 5: WHY THE LIVER (4:59 to 6:22)
3D footage only in this scene.

The answer starts with how a shark stays afloat. Most fish float using a swim bladder, a small bag of gas
inside the body. A shark uses something else to stay up: a huge, oily liver. In a great white, that liver
makes up between a quarter and a third of the whole body weight. Most of it is fat.

The shark burns that fat on long trips across the ocean, the way an engine burns fuel. For an orca, the
liver of a big great white is hundreds of kilograms of rich food in one place.

Taking only the liver may also protect the orca's teeth. Shark skin is covered in tiny scales shaped like
teeth, and it feels like sandpaper. Orcas that eat a lot of shark wear their teeth down flat. Since 1969,
three dead orcas with flat-worn teeth and shark remains in their stomachs have washed up in South
Africa. Port and Starboard seem to belong to this shark-eating type.

One quick tear gives the orca the richest part of the meal with very little chewing. The great whites of
Gansbaai answered with a move of their own.

SCENE 6: THE EMPTY SEA (6:22 to 7:59)
They left. Towner's team went through five and a half years of boat sightings and signals from tagged
sharks. Fourteen tagged great whites were tracked leaving Gansbaai when the orcas were near.

After the first attacks, single sharks stayed away for weeks. Later, the gaps grew to months. Sightings kept
dropping for two and a half years. On many days the boats went out and came back without seeing a
single great white.

Towner compared it to wild dogs on the African plains, which keep away from places where lions hunt.
The fear of being hunted was enough to move them.

[B-roll] IMAGE: Map or aerial photo of Southeast Farallon Island off San Francisco.
Keywords: "Southeast Farallon Island aerial", "Farallon Islands map San Francisco"

Scientists had already seen this on the other side of the world. On the second of November 2009, orcas
passed Southeast Farallon Island near San Francisco. They stayed for about two and a half hours.
Seventeen tagged great whites were feeding around the island that day. Within eight hours, every one of
them had gone. Sixteen stayed away for the rest of the season.

In years when orcas visited, the sharks there killed far fewer elephant seals, sometimes only a seventh of
a normal year. Back in October 1997, people on that island had watched an orca kill a great white and
eat only its liver.

In South Africa, though, no one had yet filmed the moment an orca caught a great white. That changed
in 2022.

SCENE 7: CAUGHT ON CAMERA (7:59 to 9:51)
[B-roll] VIDEO: The real drone footage of orcas hunting great white sharks off Mossel
Bay, May 2022. Keywords: "orcas kill great white shark drone Mossel Bay 2022",
"Christiaan Stopforth orca drone footage", "orcas hunting great white aerial"

On the sixteenth of May 2022, drone pilot Christiaan Stopforth was flying over Mossel Bay, further east
along the coast. Below him, five orcas were chasing a great white shark. One of the five orcas was
Starboard.

The chase lasted about an hour. Twice, a shark tried a trick that seals use against sharks. It swam in tight
circles close to an orca, keeping the hunter in sight. Five orcas were too many, because the shark could
only watch one at a time.

Then the drone filmed an orca biting into a shark right where the liver sits. Scientists think three more
sharks died that day. Four of the five orcas had never been linked to shark hunting before. Orcas learn
from one another, so the researchers warned that the skill could spread.

Before the hunt, survey boats in Mossel Bay saw great whites on every trip. In the 45 days after it, they
saw one.

A year later, Starboard showed what he could do alone. On the eighteenth of June 2023, researchers
watched him catch a young great white about 2.5 metres long. He grabbed it by the side fin. Within two
minutes he had torn it open and taken the liver. Port stayed about a hundred metres away the whole
time. A second dead great white, 3.5 metres long, was found nearby that day. Great whites then kept
away from that part of the bay for about four months.

With the great whites gone, other animals began to take their place.

SCENE 8: A COAST REARRANGED (9:51 to 11:19)
At Gansbaai, a smaller shark called the bronze whaler moved into the bay. Great whites eat bronze
whalers, so for years the smaller sharks had kept their distance. Today, cage-diving boats show their
guests bronze whalers on most trips.

[B-roll] VIDEO: Real footage of a great white leaping out of the water after a seal at Seal
Island, False Bay. Keywords: "great white shark breach Seal Island False Bay", "flying
shark False Bay seal", "Air Jaws Seal Island breach"

In False Bay the change went further. Seal Island there was once famous for great whites leaping clear of
the water after seals. Shark scientist Neil Hammerschlag and his team have surveyed the island since
2000. Their boats recorded no great whites after August 2018. On the beaches of Cape Town, shark
spotters logged more than 300 great white sightings in 2011. By 2019 they were logging none.

Sevengill sharks, which earlier surveys had never recorded at the island, began to appear. Reported
sightings of Cape fur seals around the bay went up about six times. The fish that seals eat became harder
to find, and so did the small sharks that sevengills hunt.

[B-roll] IMAGE: Real photo of the sevengill shark carcasses on the beach near Gansbaai,
February 2023. Keywords: "sevengill sharks washed up Gansbaai 2023", "dead sevengill
sharks beach orca", "Marine Dynamics sevengill carcasses"

Port and Starboard, meanwhile, kept hunting. In February 2023, about twenty sharks washed up near
Gansbaai after a single visit by the pair. Nearly all were sevengills, each with its liver removed.

At this point, the case against the two orcas looks closed. Several shark scientists think it is missing a
second suspect.

SCENE 9: THE SECOND SUSPECT (11:19 to 13:14)
In 2023, a team that included Alison Kock and Alison Towner studied great white records from the whole
South African coast. They found that numbers looked steady overall. Sightings had fallen in the west and
risen in the east. At Algoa Bay, shore anglers caught six great whites in 2013 and 59 in 2019. To that
team, it looked as if many sharks had moved east, away from the orcas.

In 2024, another group of scientists pushed back. Shark scientist Enrico Gennari and his co-authors said
great white sightings in False Bay had started falling around 2012. That was about three years before Port
and Starboard were first seen hunting there.

[B-roll] VIDEO: Real footage of shark nets and floats off a KwaZulu-Natal swimming
beach. Keywords: "shark nets KwaZulu-Natal beach", "KZN Sharks Board nets drumlines",
"shark net buoys Durban"

This group pointed to people. Shark nets and baited hooks protect swimming beaches in KwaZulu-Natal
province. Between 1978 and 2018, that gear caught 1,317 great whites, and 1,108 of them died. That
works out to about 28 sharks a year. Orcas, by comparison, are known to have killed eight great whites
since 2017.

Fishing boats that target smaller sharks add to the pressure. They take away food that great whites rely
on, and their long lines of hooks sometimes catch great whites too. South Africa's whole great white
population is small, with estimates from a few hundred to about a thousand. These scientists estimate
that losing a few dozen a year can push a population that size into decline.

So who is right? The evidence suggests both played a part. Orcas caused the sudden exits, when a busy
bay went quiet within days. Years of losses to nets and fishing meant fewer sharks were left to come
back.

SCENE 10: OTHER OCEANS (13:14 to 14:15)
3D footage only in this scene.

Orcas in other oceans have learned the same hunt. In the Gulf of California, a group known as
Moctezuma's pod was filmed killing young great whites in August 2020, and again in August 2022. Those
orcas push a shark to the surface and roll it onto its back. A shark held upside down goes limp, a state
called tonic immobility. While it lies still, the orcas take the liver.

Every shark in those hunts was young. Biologist Salvador Jorgensen, who led the Farallon study, was part
of that team. He says young sharks may simply have no experience of orcas yet.

In Australia, a 4.7 metre great white washed up near Portland, Victoria, in October 2023. Its liver was
gone. Scientists swabbed the biggest bite wound and found orca DNA.

Then, in 2026, there was news from South Africa.

SCENE 11: THE RETURN (14:15 to 15:45)
[B-roll] VIDEO: Real footage of the young great white filmed near Seal Island, False Bay,
in September 2026. Keywords: "great white shark False Bay September 2026", "white
shark Seal Island 2026 sighting", "great white returns False Bay"

On the twenty-third of September 2026, a tour boat filmed a young great white just north of Seal Island
in False Bay. The crew said it was the first they had seen there in about eight years. Six weeks later, on
the third of November, two great whites swam up to a cage-diving boat off Gansbaai. They were the
company's first in more than a year.

The people who saw them chose their words with care. A few sharks are a hopeful sign. It is too early to
call it a return. Port and Starboard are still on this coast, too. Both were reported around the Cape in
early 2026, sometimes swimming apart.

So, did two orcas empty a great white shark coast? At Gansbaai and Mossel Bay, they did, more than
once, and cameras recorded it. Across the wider coast, they had help. Nets and fishing lines were taking
great whites long before the orcas arrived.

One question is still open. Scientists do not know yet whether a great white is born knowing to flee from
orcas. It may be something each shark has to learn. What do you think? Tell us in the comments. And if
you want more videos like this, subscribe.
